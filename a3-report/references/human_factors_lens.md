# Human Factors / Resilience lens for Five-Whys & Deep Analysis

A 30-point analytical lens (in the tradition of Dekker, Hollnagel, Woods —
*Field Guide to Understanding Human Error*, Safety-II, resilience engineering).
Use it two ways:

- **Five-Whys (section 4):** frame every cause as a property of the *system /
  situation*, expressed through the actor's **local rationality** — never as a
  person's "carelessness" or "lack of awareness". An error is the **start** of
  the analysis (a symptom), not the conclusion.
- **Deep Analysis (section 5):** this is where the lens earns its keep. Combine
  **web research** with whichever of the 30 points below **hit** the situation,
  and write them as the user's **Unknown Unknowns (◆)** — the blind spots they
  cannot see because they are inside the system. Prefer the 2–4 lenses that most
  change the plan; tag each with the lens it comes from so the insight is
  traceable. Don't force all 30; surface the ones that genuinely apply.

The lens applies to any A3, not only safety incidents — read "the actor" as
whoever's behavior the A3 examines (the user, a customer, a screener, a team).

---

## 1. Remove blame on "the person" (avoid hindsight bias)
Ask: *"Why did it make sense to this person, at that moment, to do what they did?"*

1. Is the framing blaming a person? (Don't conclude "X's carelessness / lack of awareness" as the cause.)
2. Hindsight bias? (Are we judging past action as "stupid" using knowledge of the outcome we have now?)
3. Did we understand the actor's **local rationality**? (Why did the action feel reasonable to them at the time?)
4. Escaping into "lack of ~"? (Don't compare to an ideal that never existed; look at what actually happened.)
5. Is the error treated as a **result**, not a cause? (Error is the start of analysis, not the goal.)
6. Did past success become a trap? (Did the "usual way" that normally works backfire this one time?)

## 2. Expose the constraints of context (situation / environment)
Human behavior is shaped by environment; analyze the "situational pressure".

7. Was there a **double bind**? (Contradictory demands like "go faster but don't drop quality".)
8. Could the environment detect drift? (When the system slides toward danger, was there a signal to catch it?)
9. Were trade-offs dumped on the individual? (Safety vs cost vs speed left too much to on-the-spot discretion.)
10. Where were the resource limits? (Did shortage of time, people, equipment, or information force the action?)
11. Did the anomaly hide inside "business as usual"? (Did the abnormal blend into the everyday scenery?)
12. How were past **near-misses** handled? (Were they shrugged off as "OK in the end", leaving the weakness in place?)

## 3. Technology / tool mismatch (the automation trap)
Tools help but create new error forms (opacity).

13. Was the system a **"silent scaffolding"**? (Automation quietly compensates, then suddenly dumps the problem on the human at its limit.)
14. **Data isolated**? (The information existed but was scattered, hard to read as one meaningful story.)
15. **Mode error**? (Was it easy for the human to misread which state/mode the system was in?)
16. **Alarm flood**? (So many low-value warnings that the important one became "cry wolf".)
17. Did the tool **raise** cognitive load? (Extra thinking steps / screen-hops just to operate or check.)
18. **System opacity**? (Was the machine/system a black box about what it was doing inside?)

## 4. Team / organization / communication gaps
Errors form in the gaps between people.

19. Fell into a **"responsibility gap"**? (A boundary zone between teams A and B that nobody clearly owned.)
20. **Psychological safety**? (Could people raise "something's wrong" without hesitation?)
21. **Shared mental model** breakdown? (Did members' understanding of the situation or goal diverge?)
22. Did the org run on **hollow procedures**? (Following the manual literally never finishes the job, so workarounds are the daily norm.)
23. Was organizational **distance** too great? (Decision-makers far from the field issuing orders that don't fit reality.)
24. Did **knowledge update / training** keep up with the changing system?

## 5. Resilience (adaptive capacity) and its limits
Look not only at "why it failed" but at how the everyday adaptation that usually holds finally broke down.

25. Did the field's **workaround / flexible adaptation** backfire? (Humans usually cover for an incomplete system, until the cover exceeded its limit.)
26. Were early signals processed as **"just noise"**? (Did organizational inertia wave away the first faint sense of wrongness?)
27. Where were the **defenses (safeguards)** weak? (Why did the supposed double/triple checks fail to stop it midway?)
28. Was the **definition of "safe" too narrow**? (Overtrusting "following the rules = safe", failing to suspect the rule itself.)
29. Can we trace the **fragility chain**? (Was the system designed so one small miss easily triggers the next?)
30. Do countermeasures avoid relying on **"human effort"**? (Not "be more careful / train harder" but actual changes to the system or environment.)
